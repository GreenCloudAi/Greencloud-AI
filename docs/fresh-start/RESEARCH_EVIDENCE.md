# GreenCloud AI — research and source register

**Review date:** 26 November 2026. This is a bounded review for the AWS-first prototype, not a claim that every cited paper has been exhaustively replicated. Links point to publishers, author manuscripts, standards bodies, or AWS documentation. “Full text” means the paper is openly available and substantive methods/results were inspected where stated; “abstract/metadata” means conclusions must remain narrow. Reported percentages belong to their authors' datasets.

## Review method

Search targets were cloud cost optimization, QoS-aware resource sizing, workload forecasting, carbon-aware scheduling, billing normalization, and post-change verification. Inclusion required a traceable DOI or official specification, an identifiable method, and relevance to at least one GreenCloud decision. Preference went to publisher pages and author manuscripts. The source was checked for its workload, baseline, and limitations before turning it into a design choice. Provider documentation was checked against the current API/data semantics because those change over time.

The existing `report.md` is useful background but contains broad architecture claims and references that should be verified before reuse. This register is the evidence trail for the narrower first release.

### Cross-paper conclusions

The strongest shared principle is to optimize **under workload constraints**, and to evaluate on the target workload. Graph methods, ML forecasting, and carbon scheduling solve different problems; none is a universal “AI optimizer.” The first GreenCloud algorithm should use deterministic compatibility and safety filters plus explicit cost calculation. Only introduce a learned model after a time-split evaluation shows a decision improvement over a simple baseline. Carbon scheduling should wait for a flexible batch workload and defensible intensity data. A measured outcome after a change is a product requirement, but the reviewed papers alone do not prove that GreenCloud will produce savings.

### Papers needing deeper reading before corresponding features

Before adding a forecasting model, read the full Saxena benchmark and the Nawrocki/Osypanka method and reproduce a simple baseline. Before adding carbon scheduling, read and reproduce CarbonScaler on a suitable batch trace. Before citing any specific percentage from the HPC FinOps or OPTIC work, obtain the full papers and verify denominator, baseline, and experimental design. These are research tasks, not blockers for the deterministic first release.

## Open research questions and tests

1. **Data availability:** Can the selected lab account enable CUR 2.0 with resource IDs and obtain memory plus an application SLO? Record permissions and actual lag.
2. **Attribution:** Which AWS charges remain unallocated at resource level, and what policy, if any, can allocate them without false precision?
3. **Cost comparison:** For one EC2 candidate, how close is a versioned price model to the relevant effective billed cost after discounts, commitments, and usage changes?
4. **Safety:** Which workload rules actually reject unsafe options? Test bursty traffic, scheduled jobs, autoscaling, storage dependencies, and missing data.
5. **Outcome inference:** Can a lab change be measured with matched demand, or is the result inconclusive because of billing lag and confounders?
6. **Carbon:** Does a reasonable range of energy/grid assumptions change the ranking? If yes, present the range rather than one value.
7. **ML value:** Does a forecast improve action precision or realized outcomes over a simple seasonal baseline? If not, keep the simpler approach.

## Citation and claim policy for future work

For each result in a report or UI, attach source ID, collection time, observation window, method version, and coverage. Cite papers for what they tested, not for a broader product claim. Record all assumptions and corrections in this register. Recheck provider documentation when implementing because prices, export schemas, and carbon methodologies can change.

## Optimization evidence for the multi-cloud roadmap

Reviewed on 27 November 2026. This comparison covers representative primary studies. Results measure the authors’ workloads and baselines. GreenCloud requires its own evaluation before adopting an algorithm or projecting results.

| Study and source | Method, data and comparison | Reported outcome | Design implication and limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delimitrou and Kozyrakis, Quasar, ACM ASPLOS 2014. Methods and evaluation inspected. [Author paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/delimitrou/papers/2014.asplos.quasar.pdf) | Classifies performance across resource amounts, types and interference levels, then assigns resources under QoS constraints. Evaluated batch and latency-sensitive workloads on a 40-server local cluster and a dedicated 200-server EC2 cluster. Measured cluster utilization and workload throughput or latency against alternative schemes. | Reported a 47% relative utilization improvement at steady state under high load on the EC2 cluster while meeting workload constraints. | Use workload constraints and compatibility before ranking. Dedicated test clusters exclude external workload interference and the result is neither a billing saving nor a guarantee for modern instance families. |
| Venkataraman et al., Ernest, USENIX NSDI 2016. Methods and evaluation inspected. [USENIX paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/nsdi16/nsdi16-paper-venkataraman.pdf) | Fits performance models from selected small-scale runs using experimental design. Predicts completion time for analytics workloads including Spark MLlib, genomics and speech, then compares prediction error and training overhead against measured EC2 runs. | Authors report average prediction error below 20% and training overhead below 5% for long-running jobs in their evaluation. | Trial measurements can inform candidate estimates for repeatable batch jobs. The workload model and extrapolation error require revalidation; this does not predict interactive request latency. |
| Rzadca et al., Autopilot, ACM EuroSys 2020. Methods and evaluation inspected. [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/autopilot-workload-autoscaling-at-google-scale/) · [Author paper](https://www.john.e-wilkes.com/papers/2020-EuroSys-Autopilot.pdf) | Uses historical jobs, smoothed usage windows and a learning-based variant to tune task resources in Borg. Evaluates slack, throttling and out-of-memory events against Google’s internal operating baselines. | The compared ML-managed jobs had 23% relative slack versus 46% for the manually-managed soft-limit group. | Track memory pressure and CPU throttling separately from mean utilization. Borg’s controls, job mix and failure tolerance differ from customer VMs and Kubernetes clusters. Do not infer cost savings from slack alone. |
| Wang et al., Autothrottle, USENIX NSDI 2024. Proceedings abstract inspected. [USENIX proceedings](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi24/presentation/wang-zibo) | Application-wide feedback sets throttling targets for per-service controllers. Three microservice applications were evaluated on production-derived workload traces, comparing CPU use under end-to-end latency objectives. | Authors report CPU savings up to 26.21% versus their strongest baseline. | Separate application latency evidence from infrastructure counters. This supports the architecture of a later feedback loop, with full method review and reproduction required before adopting the controller. |
| Makridakis, Spiliotis and Assimakopoulos, M4 Competition, International Journal of Forecasting 2020. Evaluation tables and scoring inspected. [Publisher article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2019.04.014) | Benchmarks 61 methods on 100,000 time series with multiple cadences. Measures point accuracy using sMAPE, MASE and a relative benchmark rank, and evaluates prediction intervals. | The winning method’s overall weighted average was 0.821 against the Naïve 2 reference of 1.0. | Use per-horizon, time-ordered backtests and interval calibration. | Sukprasert et al., carbon-aware workload shifting, author preprint. Abstract and evaluated scope inspected. ### Research gaps and product experiments

- **Cost attribution across providers.** Pricing models and resource-level billing differ. Build fixtures with signed adjustments, commitments, shared costs, restored partitions and payer/member overlap. Reconcile to source totals by currency, metric and period before evaluating savings. Treat FinOps allocation and FOCUS semantics as standards guidance, separate from optimization experiments. [FinOps Allocation](https://framework.finops.org/framework/capabilities/allocation/)
- **False positives from partial telemetry.** Replay memory-bound, bursty, network-constrained and scaled workloads. Compare a CPU-only screen, a deterministic multi-signal filter, and provider-native recommendations. Measure unsafe-candidate acceptance, abstention, applicable coverage and review precision. Use documented provider requirements as a comparison point. [AWS metrics and lookback](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/compute-optimizer/latest/ug/metrics.html)
- **Business-value forecasts.** Backtest spend and demand independently with rolling origins. Report error and empirical interval coverage by workload class, with outliers and new launches isolated. Compare seasonal naïve, moving average and existing Holt before evaluating more complex models.
- **Attribution after a change.** Compare matched demand and, when available, a control or canary. Log concurrent deploys, tariff revisions and traffic shifts. Test whether results remain stable across credible baselines and wait for settled billing. [Google SRE canarying](https://sre.google/workbook/canarying-releases/)
- **Carbon and optimization evaluation.** Replay eligible flexible jobs against a simple time-of-day baseline. Measure deadline violations, cost and modeled emissions together. Record the region factor and its sensitivity. Avoid generalizing one batch scheduling result to interactive services.

This is a targeted evidence review. An exhaustive survey, full-text replication for the abstract-only entries, and system-specific validation remain open.
