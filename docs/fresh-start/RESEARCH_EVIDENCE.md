# GreenCloud AI — research and source register

**Review date:** 26 November 2026. This is a bounded review for the AWS-first prototype, not a claim that every cited paper has been exhaustively replicated. Links point to publishers, author manuscripts, standards bodies, or AWS documentation. “Full text” means the paper is openly available and substantive methods/results were inspected where stated; “abstract/metadata” means conclusions must remain narrow. Reported percentages belong to their authors' datasets.

## Review method

Search targets were cloud cost optimization, QoS-aware resource sizing, workload forecasting, carbon-aware scheduling, billing normalization, and post-change verification. Inclusion required a traceable DOI or official specification, an identifiable method, and relevance to at least one GreenCloud decision. Preference went to publisher pages and author manuscripts. The source was checked for its workload, baseline, and limitations before turning it into a design choice. Provider documentation was checked against the current API/data semantics because those change over time.

The existing `report.md` is useful background but contains broad architecture claims and references that should be verified before reuse. This register is the evidence trail for the narrower first release.

### Cross-paper conclusions

The strongest shared principle is to optimize **under workload constraints**, and to evaluate on the target workload. Graph methods, ML forecasting, and carbon scheduling solve different problems; none is a universal “AI optimizer.” The first GreenCloud algorithm should use deterministic compatibility and safety filters plus explicit cost calculation. Only introduce a learned model after a time-split evaluation shows a decision improvement over a simple baseline. Carbon scheduling should wait for a flexible batch workload and defensible intensity data. A measured outcome after a change is a product requirement, but the reviewed papers alone do not prove that GreenCloud will produce savings.

### Papers needing deeper reading before corresponding features

Before adding a forecasting model, read the full Saxena benchmark and the Nawrocki/Osypanka method and reproduce a simple baseline. Before adding carbon scheduling, read and reproduce CarbonScaler on a suitable batch trace. Before citing any specific percentage from the HPC FinOps or OPTIC work, obtain the full papers and verify denominator, baseline, and experimental design. These are research tasks, not blockers for the deterministic first release.

## Open research questions and tests

1. **Data availability:** Can the selected lab account enable CUR 2.0 with resource IDs and obtain memory plus an application SLO? Record permissions and actual lag.
2. **Attribution:** Which AWS charges remain unallocated at resource level, and what policy, if any, can allocate them without false precision?
3. **Cost comparison:** For one EC2 candidate, how close is a versioned price model to the relevant effective billed cost after discounts, commitments, and usage changes?
4. **Safety:** Which workload rules actually reject unsafe options? Test bursty traffic, scheduled jobs, autoscaling, storage dependencies, and missing data.
5. **Outcome inference:** Can a lab change be measured with matched demand, or is the result inconclusive because of billing lag and confounders?
6. **Carbon:** Does a reasonable range of energy/grid assumptions change the ranking? If yes, present the range rather than one value.
7. **ML value:** Does a forecast improve action precision or realized outcomes over a simple seasonal baseline? If not, keep the simpler approach.

## Citation and claim policy for future work

For each result in a report or UI, attach source ID, collection time, observation window, method version, and coverage. Cite papers for what they tested, not for a broader product claim. Record all assumptions and corrections in this register. Recheck provider documentation when implementing because prices, export schemas, and carbon methodologies can change.
